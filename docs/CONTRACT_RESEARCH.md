# Real-world contract and transfer research

Researched 21 September 2026 in response to Jordan's request for realistic bonuses, buy-backs and buy-outs. This is an evidence and implementation-status record. It does not replace the living GDD or approve every proposed extension. Historical examples demonstrate possible structures, not universal rules or current player terms.

## Verified structures and their game implications

| Structure | Evidence | Implication for Club Chairman |
|---|---|---|
| Buy-back right | [Juventus, Morata, 19 July 2014](https://www.juventus.com/en/news/articles/morata-signs-for-juventus): €20m initial fee over three years; Real Madrid could reacquire in two specified future seasons, at predetermined prices up to €30m linked to appearances. | Store the entitled club, activation windows, expiry and price schedule. The right is not available to every bidder. |
| First refusal / matching rights | [Barcelona, Jan Virgili, 28 August 2025](https://www.fcbarcelona.com/en/news/4351743/agreement-for-the-transfer-of-jan-virgili-to-mallorca) confirms a future-sale percentage and first refusal, without publishing the percentage or full wording. [Daniel Geey's first-hand legal explanation](https://www.danielgeey.com/done-deal-blog/football-transfers-buy-back-clauses-explained) distinguishes buy-back pricing from the opportunity to respond to a sale the current club is willing to accept. | Model notification, response deadline and comparable offer terms separately from a fixed buy-back. Do not invent undisclosed real-deal amounts. |
| Buy-out versus transfer release | [Spain's Real Decreto 1006/1985, article 16](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1985-12313#a16) concerns compensation when a professional sportsperson terminates voluntarily without cause attributable to the club; absent agreement, the labour court determines compensation. [Barcelona's Ansu Fati announcement, 4 December 2019](https://www.fcbarcelona.com/en/football/barca-b/news/1515242/fc-barcelona-improves-ansu-fatis-contract-and-raises-his-buyout-clause) describes an agreed amount increasing from €170m to €400m on signing a first-team professional contract. | Keep player termination/compensation distinct from a club obligation to accept a qualifying transfer bid. Country rules and exact wording matter. Do not assume every country mandates a fixed buy-out amount. |
| Conditional loan purchase | [Juventus's Locatelli disclosure, 18 August 2021](https://www.juventus.com/images/image/upload/fl_attachment/dev/w2m7cipoo3gyt3nllogi.pdf): temporary registration to June 2023; purchase obligation conditional on sporting objectives; €25m payable over three years, plus up to €12.5m of further performance bonuses. The exact sporting triggers are not disclosed there. | An option permits purchase; an obligation commits purchase only when its written condition is satisfied. Store permanent employment consent and funding commitments. Do not invent Locatelli's precise trigger. |
| Transfer add-ons | [Manchester United 2025 annual report, note 31, PDF page 150](https://ir.manutd.com/~/media/Files/M/Manutd-IR/documents/2025-mu-plc-form-20-f.pdf) lists contingent transfer amounts associated with club appearances, team success, new contracts, international appearances, awards and future transfers. | Separate the guaranteed fee from capped conditional amounts. Earning a payment and paying it are different events; earned debt survives departure. |
| Gross versus profit sell-on | The Jan Virgili announcement refers to a percentage of a future sale; [Barcelona's Sergi Domínguez announcement, 30 June 2025](https://www.fcbarcelona.com/en/news/4299183/agreement-with-dinamo-zagreb-for-the-transfer-of-sergi-dominguez) expressly refers to future profits. | Specify the calculation base and deductions. A share of a future fee is not interchangeable with a share of profit. |
| Signing and loyalty payments | United's same annual report, accounting policy 2.5, PDF page 108, describes signing fees paid in annual instalments. It distinguishes loyalty payments for past service from payments for ongoing service; some past-service renewal payments have no future-service requirement or clawback. | Signing fees need not all be immediate. A loyalty label alone does not establish whether a player forfeits money by leaving: persist the actual earning conditions. |
| Player and squad bonuses | [Richard Berry's legal analysis, 16 April 2014](https://www.lawinsport.com/topics/features/item/bonus-structures-in-english-professional-football) describes starts/substitute distinctions, goals, clean sheets, dated loyalty payments, appearance-linked wage rises and squad success pools allocated by participation. | Define eligible competitions, starts/minutes, thresholds, payment dates and caps. Individual bonuses and a shared team pool need separate accounting. This historical analysis is evidence of structures, not a current rulebook. |

Geey also explains that a buy-back still needs personal terms and a willing player. A clause between clubs must not silently transfer a player's employment. Cash, consent, medical and registration checks remain separate gates.

## Current implementation status

**Already present before 0.21:** fixed fees and dated deferred payments, signing fees, negotiated wages, appearance/goal bonuses, club extension options, gross/profit next-sale rights, basic loans and recall rules.

**Implemented locally for 0.21:** fixed transfer-release amounts; player extension options; annual wage increases; relegation wage cuts; one employment promotion bonus; appearance/promotion transfer add-ons; loan purchase options and appearance-conditioned obligations; persistent earned debt; related controls, consent and financial forecasts. These are explicit development rules, not a complete recreation of national law. A release amount in this build is a transfer condition, not the Spanish player-termination mechanism.

**Researched, not implemented:** fixed/stepped buy-backs; first refusal and offer matching; country-specific buy-outs; loyalty schedules and deferred signing fees; richer individual/team bonuses; international/award/qualification add-ons; appearance-triggered extensions; robust risk-based AI negotiation. The first extension I recommend is buy-back rights with dated prices, expiry and fresh personal consent, followed by matching rights and country-specific release/buy-out behaviour.

The current compact loan calendar is also a limitation: binding purchase must settle within the current registration window. Full-season loans and future-window conditional registrations require calendar/registration work. Real-world examples do not justify claiming that gap is complete.

## Proposed acceptance checks for the next extension

- Each right shows who can use it, against which club, on which dates, at what price, and what ends it. Renewal, third-club resale and overlapping sell-on rights have explicit outcomes.
- A buy-back can require the selling club to cooperate while the player can still refuse personal terms. Missing a deadline expires the right without spending money.
- A matching-right response compares the whole offer, including instalments and contingent amounts. A simple headline-fee comparison is insufficient.
- Bonus wording states whether a substitute appearance counts, which competition counts, whether thresholds repeat, and the maximum total payable. Unearned possibilities remain separate from earned liabilities and guaranteed commitments.
- AI clubs value the whole package and respect their own cash and wage limits. Adding an enormous bonus must not produce an unpriced claim against a rival.

Illustrative game example, not a real contract: sell a prospect for £500,000, retain a £1m buy-back in the next summer window and a £1.25m price the following summer, plus a separately defined profit sell-on. The UI should show how exercising the right interacts with that sell-on before consent; it must not silently double-charge or net amounts without an agreed rule.
